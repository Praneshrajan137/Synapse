### SRC-MU-01 - ashwin-damera-eruditus-intelligence-report.md

- **Origin:** Pratham Mittal of Masters' Union interviews Ashwin Damera, co-founder and chief
  executive of the education company Eruditus.
- **Thesis:** Higher education is slow and trusts brands, so an education company scales through

### SRC-MU-02 - eric_ries_think_school_intelligence_report.md

- **Pin:** `184df963cb3a`, 23,390 bytes. Type: report.
- **Origin:** Ganesh Prasad of Think School interviews Eric Ries, author of *The Lean Startup* and
  co-founder of Answer AI.
- **Thesis:** A start-up is a sequence of experiments; spend only on what shortens the loop from
  building to measuring to learning, and judge by behaviour, not opinion.
- **Worth keeping:**
  - Talking to customers produces hypotheses; measuring what they do tests them. People who say
    they would buy find reasons not to when asked to pay. Q-01's interviews will produce
    hypotheses, and only a store's behaviour, such as following shadow recommendations, tests
    them: INS-018, INS-022.
  - His costliest mistake: a plan forecast six months of no revenue and then a sharp rise. At six
    months the team, still at zero, called itself on plan, and the rise never came. A forecast
    that predicts nothing for a period cannot be wrong during it; say in advance what result by
    when would show the plan is failing: INS-002, INS-033.
  - Money that shortens the loop from building to learning is well spent and the rest is waste:
    INS-020.
  - In retail, the number of stores is a vanity measure and profit per store the real one:
    INS-056.
  - He asks candidates when their favourite best practice would be a terrible idea. Each of
    SYNAPSE's gates could state the same about itself: INS-014.
- **Not kept:** the founder-cost argument, the growth-engine taxonomy, the brand-building and
  hiring frameworks, the case studies of retailers' generosity and the sponsor segment.
- **Relevance to SYNAPSE: 1.**

### SRC-MU-04 - reddit_goldmine_intelligence_report.md

- **Pin:** `9f0c71256015`, 24,344 bytes. Type: report.
- **Origin:** a Masters' Union session with Sahil Goel, co-founder and chief executive of
  Shiprocket, an Indian e-commerce shipping platform.
- **Thesis:** India is thousands of distinct local markets; durable brands come from niche
  problems found in customers' public complaints, sold through channels the brand controls.
- **Worth keeping:**
  - Find ideas in what customers complain about in public, not in what founders like, and use
    models to mine those complaints. Public complaints about quick-commerce stockouts, wrong
    substitutions and missing items are evidence of where replenishment fails the customer, the
    pull INS-059 asks for: R-60, INS-018.
  - Advertising return should be measured on delivered orders, not placed ones. The outcome is the
    one that reaches the customer: INS-056.
  - India is many local markets that differ by language, region and spending, and cannot be run
    as one chain. Demand differs by locality as well as by item, which a twin of one or two
    cities with an even mix cannot show: INS-060, R-53.
  - Neighbourhood grocers are good at local credit, forecasting what their customers will want and
    knowing them personally; he would equip them rather than replace them. A candidate user for
    Q-01, and a reminder that the people being automated already forecast (own reading): INS-058,
    Q-01.
  - Commit to the problem, not the product, and the product can change many times: T-004.
- **Not kept:** the company's pivots, the macroeconomic figures, the cash-on-delivery economics,
  the founder-brand remarks and the ninety-day business exercise.
- **Relevance to SYNAPSE: 1.** It is the folder's only source on Indian retail operations.

### SRC-MU-05 - startup-masterclass-analysis.md

- **Pin:** `ec54a6ed1729`, 17,721 bytes. Type: report.
- **Origin:** a Masters' Union session with Ashish Kashyap, founder of ibibo, Goibibo and

### SRC-MU-07 - tech_leadership_dual_master_report.md

- **Pin:** `7c95e58fba36`, 34,980 bytes. Type: report.
- **Origin:** two talks in one report: Qasar Younis, co-founder and chief executive of Applied
  Intuition and a former chief operating officer of Y Combinator, and Dennis Woodside, chief
  executive of Freshworks.
- **Thesis:** Younis: build the business model into the technology, prefer a horizontal layer
  over a vertical bet, and write down the reasoning for every decision. Woodside: at scale, attend
  only to decisions large enough to move the company.
- **Worth keeping:**
  - The self-driving start-ups of 2017 took technical risk and market risk at once by building the
    whole vehicle stack, and most failed. Applied Intuition sold simulation and tooling to every
    maker instead. A simulation and proof layer, sold horizontally, outlived the vertical bets it
    served; that is the strongest precedent in the corpus for T-004's harness-as-product reading.
  - The business model has to be designed into the technology from the start; adding it later is
    like plumbing a finished house. Q-03's answer shapes the architecture, not only the
    marketing: Q-03.
  - Every decision of consequence is written down with its reasoning, so that when an outcome
    differs the faulty premise can be found. SYNAPSE's ADRs do this; the habit is worth keeping
    for operational choices too: INS-002, INS-034.
  - Real fit is customers giving time, paying and running the product in production, and it is
    re-earned continuously; build where demand is already urgent: INS-056, INS-059.
- **Not kept:** the founder histories, the remarks on co-founders, fear and reading lists, and all
  of Woodside's talk on scale, executive hiring and the chief executive's role.
- **Relevance to SYNAPSE: 1.**

## What the folder says as a whole

1. **Behaviour tests a hypothesis; conversation only generates one,** and a plan that predicts
   nothing for months cannot fail during them (cards 02, 07).
2. **A tool built to solve the company's own problem can become the product,** and a horizontal
   simulation layer outlived the vertical companies it served (05, 07).
3. **The product is the outcome the customer feels,** measured on delivered orders and production
   use, not on activity (04, 06, 07).
4. **Demand differs by locality as well as by item,** and the people a system would automate
   already forecast for their own customers (04).
5. **Opposed goals widen the options only with someone who decides between them** (06).

No figure from this folder carries a SYNAPSE decision, so no web check was made.

**Where the folder agrees with SYNAPSE as built:** written decision records (ADRs), agents with
independent objectives, and an audit that records what ran.

**Where it argues against SYNAPSE:**

- Cards 05 and 07 add two precedents to card 18 of The Neon Show: an internal fix that became a
  company, and a simulation-tooling company that outlived the vertical bets. Together they make
  the harness, not the eight agents, the more likely durable asset (T-004).
- Card 04: a twin of one or two cities with an even item mix cannot show how demand varies by
  locality, which is how Indian retail actually varies (INS-060, R-53).
- Card 06: opposed agents only help where an arbiter binds, which ADR-052 records as not yet true
  of SYNAPSE's arbitration (INS-023, R-16).
